The engine behind the ratings. Every occupation is rebuilt bottom-up from its formal ESCO work-actions. Each action is weighted by observed demand — the share of this market's ads asking for it, no hand-picked weights — and scored on AI-tool evidence: how many distinct AI providers ship tools that can do it alone (replace) vs help a human (assist), each judgment made against the action's official definition, not its name. Actions without enough evidence stay unscored — never assumed safe. Capability ≠ adoption: this measures what today's tools can do, not what firms have deployed.

How to read: the horizontal axis is how much of an occupation's in-demand work AI can touch at all (assist or automate); the vertical axis is how much it can do on its own. A dot always sits below the diagonal — AI can't replace more than it can touch — so nothing ever reads as "100% gone". The gap to the diagonal is the augmentation zone: work AI helps with but can't take over. Data-science work lands near 30% autonomous of 93% touched (~32% of the exposed work — inside the 33–44% band the Anthropic Economic Index reports for data occupations, our external sanity check).

What "does it alone" vs "assists" means — the same automation-vs-augmentation split the Anthropic Economic Index uses. For each work-action we look at every AI tool that can perform it and ask: does the tool execute the action itself (automation → replacement) or only support a person doing it (augmentation)? The whole bar = how many AI vendors can touch the action at all (its exposure); the red part = how many replace the human. An all-red bar means every capable vendor automates it — a strong signal, not missing data. Click any action for its official ESCO definition and the actual tools. Short bar = thin evidence.
