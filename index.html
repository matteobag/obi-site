Collection running · updated weekly

Everyone is guessing which skills still matter. We measure it, live.

OBI is an independent index of what the labour market is buying. In the age of AI skills date faster than anyone tracks them, so we collect job postings across fifteen countries and every sector, week after week, and measure three things: what employers ask for, what AI can already do on its own, and how far what is taught has drifted from both.

Not a survey. Not an annual report. A continuous read of what employers post, rebuilt every week — including the skills official classifications have not named yet. The index and the taxonomy behind it will both be published in full.

The problem

Human capital went volatile. The risk is unpriced.

AI is compressing the useful life of a skill from decades to years — a curriculum can lose its value while students are still enrolled, and a hiring plan can age before it is filled. Everyone downstream is making multi-year commitments against a signal nobody publishes.

Students & workersYears and savings, committed to skills nobody can tell them will still be wanted.
Universities & training providersDeciding what to teach, what to open and what to cut — on evidence that is years old.
EmployersHiring and reskilling plans set years ahead, with no weekly read on which skills are eroding.
Governments & the publicRetraining funds allocated blind, and no way to tell afterwards what they bought.

Why it's different
Weekly, not annual

The labour market changes every week. The reports about it appear once a year, and describe a market that has already moved.

Nobody pays to be rated

We're not owned by a training provider, a job board or a recruiter, and the rating itself is never for sale.

Built to be recomputed

We keep the complete postings, not just the extracts — so any number we publish can be rebuilt from the raw record, with the same code, years later.

The live index opens market by market. Pick yours.

Each market opens as its coverage reaches the level we are willing to publish. Tell us which one matters to you and we will show you where it stands — what is already running, and what is not. We would rather show you the machine than a deck.

What we don't claim. No source sees a labour market in full, so we publish coverage, shares and changes — never a count of the whole market. And nothing here is a published index yet: the pages you can open are a 2026 proof of concept on a partial sample — tech occupations, a handful of markets — frozen at that date, and each market will be published only once its coverage is high enough for us to stand behind it. AI exposure is projected capability, not observed adoption: it marks the risk zone, it does not predict the outcome. Programme ratings are directional.
